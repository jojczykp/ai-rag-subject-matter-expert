TITLE: Simple Building-Physics Calculations
TAGS: calculations, formulas, heat-loss, ventilation, solar, examples

These simplified calculations are for screening and education. Use approved methods for design and certification.

TRANSMISSION HEAT LOSS
Heat flow through a component:
Q_dot = U × A × ΔT
where Q_dot is watts, U is W/(m²·K), A is m², and ΔT is kelvins or degrees Celsius difference.
Example: U = 0.15, area = 100 m², ΔT = 20 K.
Q_dot = 0.15 × 100 × 20 = 300 W.

LINEAR THERMAL BRIDGE
Q_dot = Ψ × L × ΔT
where Ψ is W/(m·K) and L is junction length in metres.

APPROXIMATE VENTILATION HEAT FLOW
Q_dot ≈ 0.33 × airflow_m3_per_hour × ΔT
The factor is an approximate volumetric heat capacity of air in Wh/(m³·K). With heat recovery, apply the effective unrecovered fraction and include fan energy and system losses separately.

ASSEMBLY RESISTANCE
For a uniform layer:
R = thickness / conductivity
Total U is approximately 1 divided by the sum of layer and surface resistances. Repeating bridges require area-weighted or numerical calculation.

SOLAR GAIN
Solar heat gain is approximately:
Q_dot_solar = irradiance × glazed_area × g_value × shading_factor
Real modelling also includes frame fraction, incidence angle, dirt and time-varying shading.

ANNUAL ENERGY
Energy = average_power × time.
A 500 W load operating for 1,000 hours uses 500 kWh.

AIR CHANGE
n50 = airflow_at_50Pa / test_volume.
If airflow is 600 m³/h and volume is 1,000 m³, n50 = 0.6 h⁻¹.

CARBON
Operational emissions = energy_use × emission_factor.
Embodied result = quantity × product_emission_factor, summed across products and declared life-cycle stages.
Always state units and boundaries.
