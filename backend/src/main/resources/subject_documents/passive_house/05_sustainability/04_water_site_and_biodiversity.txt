TITLE: Water, Site and Biodiversity
TAGS: water, stormwater, biodiversity, landscape, heat-island

Sustainable site design protects ecological value, manages water near its source and reduces urban heat. Begin with a site survey covering habitats, mature trees, soil, drainage, contamination, flood risk and connections to walking, cycling and public transport.

Reduce potable-water demand through efficient fixtures, leak detection and appropriate appliances. Meter significant uses. In larger projects, separate meters help identify irrigation, cooling or tenant consumption.

Rainwater can serve irrigation, toilet flushing or other non-potable uses where permitted. System design must address storage, treatment, cross-connection control, overflow, maintenance and seasonal yield. Greywater reuse has additional hygiene and regulatory requirements.

Use permeable surfaces, swales, rain gardens, detention and green roofs to slow runoff and improve water quality. Infiltration depends on soil, groundwater, contamination and foundation conditions. Overflow routes should remain safe during extreme rainfall.

Select native or climate-adapted planting that supports pollinators and requires limited irrigation after establishment. Avoid invasive species. Preserve mature vegetation where possible; replacement trees take years to provide equivalent habitat and shade.

Reduce heat-island effects with shade trees, vegetated surfaces and appropriate high-reflectance materials. Reflective roofs can reduce cooling load but glare and local context should be considered.

Exterior lighting should meet safety needs while limiting upward light, spill and unnecessary operating hours. Façade glazing and landscape design should reduce bird-collision risk.

Site measures must be maintained. Provide access, sediment cleanouts, planting establishment plans and clear responsibility for long-term care.
