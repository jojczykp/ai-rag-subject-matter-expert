TITLE: Sustainable and Healthy Material Selection
TAGS: materials, health, voc, fire, durability, responsible-sourcing

Material selection should balance environmental impact, health, technical performance, durability, maintenance, fire safety, cost and availability. A single label such as natural, recycled or local does not establish overall sustainability.

Prioritise materials that fulfil several functions efficiently and last for the required service life. Avoid unnecessary finishes. Design exposed durable structure or lining where appropriate, while respecting acoustics and fire protection.

Use low-emitting products for interior paints, adhesives, sealants, flooring and furniture. Review formaldehyde and volatile organic compound emissions using relevant testing schemes. Ventilate during and after construction and keep absorptive materials dry.

Avoid hazardous substances where safer alternatives provide equivalent performance. Existing buildings may contain asbestos, lead, polychlorinated biphenyls, treated timber or contaminated soils; assessment and removal require qualified professionals.

Responsible sourcing includes legality, labour conditions, forestry management, recycled content and supply-chain transparency. Certification can support due diligence but does not replace project-specific evaluation.

Bio-based materials can offer low embodied impacts and moisture-buffering properties, but must be protected from bulk water, pests and prolonged high humidity. Fire performance and insurance requirements need early review.

Recycled content can reduce virgin extraction, yet recyclability at end of life is also important. Composite materials and permanent adhesives can make recovery difficult. Prefer reversible mechanical connections where they meet performance needs.

Durability is environmental performance. Choose replaceable weather seals, maintainable coatings, accessible membranes and robust exposed surfaces. Document cleaning agents and repair methods so maintenance does not damage products.
