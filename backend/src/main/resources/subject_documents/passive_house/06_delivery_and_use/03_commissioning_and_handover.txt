TITLE: Commissioning and Handover
TAGS: commissioning, handover, ventilation, controls, documentation

Commissioning verifies that systems are installed, adjusted and operating according to design intent. It is especially important in low-load buildings because small control or balancing errors can represent a large share of total energy use.

Review installation before startup: equipment access, filters, condensate drainage, duct cleanliness, insulation, dampers, sensors, valves, electrical protection and labels. Confirm that outdoor-air and exhaust paths are unobstructed.

Balance ventilation room by room and record measured flows. Verify normal, reduced and boost modes. Check supply and extract totals, fan power, heat-recovery operation, summer bypass and frost protection.

Test heating and cooling sequences, sensor calibration, setpoints and interlocks. Confirm that systems do not heat and cool simultaneously. Observe part-load operation, not only full output.

Verify domestic hot-water temperatures, recirculation schedules, insulation and safety controls. Check renewable generation, monitoring and battery modes where present.

Provide the owner with concise documents: system overview, setpoints, filter sizes, maintenance intervals, warranties, commissioning records, emergency instructions and contacts. Complex technical manuals should be accompanied by a short user guide.

Train occupants or facilities staff in practical tasks. Demonstrate filter replacement, boost ventilation, shading, summer operation and alarm response. Avoid telling users never to open windows.

Plan seasonal commissioning after the building has operated in different weather. Review trend data and complaints. Fine-tune schedules and setpoints while preserving indoor air quality.
