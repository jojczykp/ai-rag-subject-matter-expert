TITLE: Construction Quality Assurance
TAGS: construction, quality-assurance, inspections, mockup, sequencing

High-performance details must be buildable, visible and verifiable. Quality assurance begins with clear drawings and responsibilities, then continues through procurement, sequencing, inspection and testing.

Hold a preconstruction workshop focused on the thermal envelope, airtightness, waterproofing and ventilation. Use large-scale junction drawings and physical samples. Explain why each layer matters so trades can make informed decisions when conditions change.

Construct a representative mock-up containing a window, wall, insulation, membranes, cladding support and service penetration. Test or dismantle it if useful. Resolve sequencing before repetitive work begins.

Create inspection hold points before work is concealed. Typical checks include substrate preparation, membrane laps, taped sheathing, window perimeter seals, insulation fit, cavity closures, roof transitions and service penetrations.

Photograph completed layers with location references and scale. Photos do not replace inspection, but they provide a record for certification, maintenance and future alterations.

Protect finished work. Airtight membranes and taped boards can be damaged by storage, scaffolding, follow-on trades or weather. Mark no-fix zones and provide approved attachment routes.

Perform intermediate blower-door tests while leaks are accessible. Use zone or guarded testing for complex buildings when appropriate. Repair root causes rather than covering symptoms with surface sealant.

Coordinate substitutions. A new window, bracket, membrane or insulation product can alter thermal, fire, moisture and airtightness performance. Require technical review before purchase or installation.

Close out defects systematically and update as-built drawings. Quality is achieved through repeated verification, not a final inspection alone.
